The problem

Fraud arrives in three forms

Reused photo

A real picture from another claim, cropped, screenshotted or forwarded. The most common route.

Made or edited photo

An AI-generated image, or damage painted onto a real phone.

Receipt that does not fit

Wrong amount, date or item for the claim.

A person cannot compare a photo against every earlier claim, or tell a real photo from an AI-made one.

False hits on the 40 new photos: quick fingerprint 0, feature matching 3, SSCD 0. SSCD runs on an always-on endpoint in under a second.

What we tested and left out

Two ideas that did not earn a place

AI-image detectors

Scored 0.55 to 0.79, where 0.5 is a coin flip. One flagged nearly a third of real photos. We use a vision model as a hint only. AI-made photos are our weakest area: 7 of 8 flagged.

One learned score

No better than the rules, and on a fraud type it had not seen, recall fell from 97% to 87%. It missed every receipt mismatch. The explainable rules stay.

The heat map is a guide, not proof: it lights up the busiest parts of any photo, even a genuine one.

Honest limits

What this does not prove yet

  • Small and synthetic. 66 made-up claims over public photos. Direction, not guarantees.
  • False alarms are the main cost. 43% of honest claims get a look. Tune on real investigator labels.
  • AI-made photos are the weak spot, and new generators keep arriving.
  • Reuse is checked against earlier claims only, not the internet.
  • Lakebase from the app is sub-second, not instant: 457 ms measured end to end. Decisions save to Delta and to Lakebase.
